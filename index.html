The Residence
Offered for the first time in over a decade

A craftsman residence beside Heritage Park, moments from downtown Kirkland, with a sweeping, nearly 180-degree view from Mt. Rainier to the Olympics.

Built in 2002 in the Pacific Northwest craftsman tradition, Glassbridge unfolds across 3,580 square feet with four bedrooms and three and a half baths. A two-story entry anchored by a cantilevered glass stair and fan-shaped glass bridge, honored with a 2004 BALA Platinum award for Best Detail in a Home, sets the tone for a residence built with uncommon craft throughout.

The home sits just off Heritage Park, with a nearly 180-degree view that sweeps from the summit of Mt. Rainier, across Lake Washington and the Bellevue and Seattle skylines, to the Olympic Mountains beyond: an exposure that fills the upper floors with sunset light. Downtown Kirkland's waterfront, restaurants, and galleries are an easy walk away.

Addresses that pair this degree of walkability with unobstructed lake and skyline views rarely come to market in downtown Kirkland. Glassbridge is offered for the first time in over a decade.

Design & Craftsmanship

A BALA Platinum award for Best Detail in a Home

In January of 2004, this home received a BALA Platinum award for Best Detail in a Home. Spanning the two-story entry that bisects the home, a fan-shaped glass bridge connects the two halves of the upper floor while an open riser stair with cantilevered glass treads connects the upper and lower levels. This design makes a dramatic impact upon entering the home and showcases the inherent duality of glass, a material that is transparent yet solid: transmitting both mass and light. Well crafted, the glass bridge and stair blend well with the craftsman style of the home.

Downtown Kirkland

Downtown Kirkland is one of the Eastside's most walkable neighborhoods, a compact grid of waterfront parks, independent restaurants, wine bars, and galleries, all within a few blocks of Lake Washington. Heritage Park, just steps from the home, offers open lawn and lake access away from the busier main waterfront.

Seattle is roughly twenty minutes away via the SR-520 floating bridge, and Bellevue's business district is closer still. Both are visible from the home's upper floors, part of a nearly 180-degree panorama that takes in Mt. Rainier, Lake Washington, and the Olympic Mountains: a rare combination of lakeside, small-town character with mountains and two skylines in view, and easy access to either city.
